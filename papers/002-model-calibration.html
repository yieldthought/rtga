RTGA · Development study 002 · 5 September 2026

Small world models learn motion
before collisions

Random experience, short-horizon prediction, and the limits of ensemble agreement

Abstract

A three-member probabilistic ensemble learns useful motion predictions from 500–2,000 random-action transitions. In two small puck worlds, all 24 fitted models outperform constant-state prediction at one and eight steps; 20 do so at 24 steps. Residual error concentrates on rare contact-like transitions. Longer action holds increase spatial coverage, but coverage and ensemble agreement do not reliably establish accurate long-range dynamics.

Scope of evidence

24 fits, three seeds per condition. This is a development diagnostic, with no confirmatory significance claim.

No goal, reward, pretrained model, imagined training examples, or task-directed collection.

Methods

We cross two fixed layouts (open, rooms), uniform random action holds of 1 or 8 primitive frames, and training sets of 500 or 2,000 actual transitions. Smaller sets are prefixes of longer trajectories. Fitting budgets match; bootstrap batches are independent.

Each ensemble has three two-layer, 64-unit networks. Continuous heads predict Gaussian state changes; a Bernoulli head predicts the door flag. Checkpoints precede fitting and follow 100 and 500 optimizer batches. Evaluation uses 32 separate actual trajectories of 48 frames, from 16 initial positions, with both action-hold policies. Probe experience never enters replay.

What was held fixed

Adam 0.002; batch 128 per member; normalized state/delta scales [1,1,½,½,1,1]; log-variance bounds [−8,1]; binary loss weight 10.

Held-out starts differ from the collection start, but may overlap regions later visited. This is fixed-layout generalization.

The missing events matter

Transitions in which a moving velocity coordinate suddenly reaches zero account for around 1% of probes, yet roughly 70% of the remaining physical squared error. Their mean per-event error is about 300–350 times that of other transitions. Small aggregate loss therefore conceals events that can invalidate plans near walls.

Long-rollout position RMSE remains 0.070–0.193 per coordinate in a unit-square world. Predictive improvement is evidence of learned motion; it is not evidence that the world is mastered.

Agreement is an imperfect signal

Local disagreement and real one-step error have Spearman correlations from -0.167 to 0.496; 5 of 24 are negative. The most-disagreed decile is error-enriched in the group means, but the ranking is inconsistent.

Calibration limits

These are error-ranking checks, not probability calibration or evidence of information gain. A separate experiment must test whether selected transitions produce subsequent learning progress.

No mechanism learned

The door is always open in both layouts; the noise sensor is constant. Tiny Brier scores therefore test a constant flag. This study contains no switch event and cannot establish noisy-TV avoidance.

A bounded next experiment

Median fitting time is 68 ms for 100 batches and 335 ms for 500 on one CPU thread. The longer fit improves 24-step error in 16/24 runs and worsens it in the others. Online comparisons should count warmup and fitting, and compare planning horizons without assuming an optimum.

Prioritize real-contact prediction, generic replay sampling of high observed errors, and closed-loop control checks. Keep the evaluation stream separate. Adding hidden exact-wall constraints would no longer test the learned simulator.

Provenance
